Executive summary

The application does one job well: it turns a painted monthly calendar into a fee message on the clipboard, in about 200 ms, with zero dependencies. Nothing in this audit argues for replacing that shape.

Six conclusions dominate.

  1. Performance is not a problem and never will be at this scale. A modelled three years of use — 8 groups, 36 months, 2,304 lesson dates — occupies 70,361 bytes, which is about 1.4 % of the localStorage ceiling I measured directly on this origin: QuotaExceededError on the write after 5,177,344 characters had been stored successfully in one key. Every render completes in under 60 ms. RP-01's quota TBD is closed, with the unit caveat in section 4 M9. Do not spend effort here.
  2. What degrades with use is ergonomics, not speed. At three years of history the group modal renders 36 month rows sorted oldest-first, so the row she needs every single month is row 36 of 36 — 1,381 px of scrolling on a desktop and 2,627 px on a phone, and it is not visible when the modal opens. This is the single largest recurring time cost in the product and it gets worse every month she uses it.
  3. The app keeps no record of what it has sent. copyAndClose (index.html:901-910) writes to the clipboard and stores nothing; greps for sentAt, history and archive return zero. Across 36 visually identical month rows there is no way to tell an invoiced month from an uninvoiced one. For a monthly billing task, that is a capability gap, not a nicety.
  4. Money can go wrong quietly, and the wrongness reaches students. The price inputs carry no min or step, so a negative price produces a real generated message reading total fee of -PLN 50.00 — runtime-verified. The shipped default template's own wording produces we will have 1 lessons for any single-lesson month; RP-01 rated that defect Low on the strength of two internal UI strings, but the same wording is in the outgoing message. And at three years of history one keystroke in the bulk price box rewrote 36 months of prices in the draft — measured, not inferred.
  5. The WCAG version matters and the brief's framing needs correcting. Under WCAG 2.1 the only target-size criterion is 2.5.5 Target Size at Level AAA, requiring 44x44 CSS px [S1]; there is no target-size requirement at AA. RP-01 judged target size passing against 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) at 24x24, which is a WCAG 2.2 criterion confirmed absent from 2.1 [S1]. Both reports are right about their own version. Measured here: day cells 41.6x29 px on a phone, toolbar buttons about 29.5 px tall — an AAA gap, not an AA failure. The genuine AA failures are contrast (1.4.3, 1.4.11), status messages (4.1.3), language of parts (3.1.2) and label quality (2.4.6); the genuine Level A failures are keyboard operability (2.1.1), focus order (2.4.3), name/role/value (4.1.2), labels (3.3.2) and semantics (1.3.1).
  6. For this specific user, accessibility is the fourth priority, and I will say so. She is one sighted teacher using a mouse. The keyboard and screen-reader failures are real, cheap to fix during the migration, and block RP-03's Playwright suite — but they do not cost her time today. The three changes that do are ranked in section 7.

Three findings are new and were not in RP-01: the group-name prefill collides with the first thing she types; the phone-clipped ◀ button is unreachable even with scrolling, not merely inconvenient; and the app's one deliberate focus indicator fails 1.4.11 at 2.78:1.

7. Top three highest-leverage changes, ranked with justification

1. Make the current month reachable in one action, and mark it once it has been sent. B1 plus B2 plus B20. This is first because it is the only item whose cost is paid every month and grows monotonically. Measured at a modelled three years of use, the row she needs is row 36 of 36, requiring 1,381 px of scrolling on a desktop and 2,627 px on a phone, and it is not visible when the modal opens; at five years it is worse. Nothing about the current design gets better with time. In the same breath, sentAt answers the question the tool cannot answer today — did August go out — which is the question a billing tool exists to answer. Together these three items convert a six-step monthly chore, three steps of which are pure navigation, into two clicks from the group card.

2. Stop the app losing her work, and give her a backup she can actually restore from. B17 plus B18 plus B19. Second, not first, only because the loss is probabilistic while the scrolling cost is certain. But the expected damage is far larger: RP-01 runtime-verified that a single corrupt localStorage value produces a silently dead page with no UI route to recovery, and the app's only existing backup path omits the template, has no BOM, throws on a malformed month key it can itself create, and silently destroys existing data on a balanced stray quote. This is also the sequencing RP-05 section 6 already committed to — hardened local storage plus a versioned JSON export ships before any Firestore sync — so this item is not a competing proposal, it is the same work seen from the UX side. The backup-age indicator is the part that makes it different from the arrangement that already failed once: a nag she can see beats a discipline she has to remember.

3. Stop the money going wrong quietly. B7 plus B16 plus B10. Third because the two failures here are lower-probability than the first two but higher-embarrassment, which is the axis the brief asks me to weigh. Verified: with no min or step on the price inputs, a mistyped value produces a real outgoing message reading total fee of -PLN 50.00; the shipped default template produces we will have 1 lessons for every single-lesson month; and one keystroke in the bulk price box rewrote 36 months of prices in the draft with no indication and no undo. The first two are visible to students and parents. The third is visible to nobody, which is worse. B7 and B10 are ship-now; B16 needs one product decision first.

Why accessibility is fourth, stated plainly. B3, B4, B5, B11, B12 and B13 fix Level A and AA failures and I am not softening them: the primary task genuinely cannot be completed by keyboard, and 9 of 14 tab stops sit in invisible dialogs. But the sole end user is a sighted teacher operating a mouse, and none of that costs her a minute today. It still belongs in bucket (i) for three reasons that do not depend on her: it is at its cheapest inside a component boundary with real focus management, so doing it during the migration costs a fraction of doing it after; RP-03's Playwright suite cannot write unambiguous role-based queries while 36 buttons share one accessible name and closed dialogs stay in the tree; and a keyboard-operable calendar is the difference between a tool that works on a tablet with a keyboard case and one that does not. Ranking it fourth is a statement about this user's month, not about the work's worth.
